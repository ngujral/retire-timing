    Common questions

    Can I start CPP or OAS at 71?

    No. CPP can start from 60 to 70 and OAS from 65 to 70. Increases stop at 70, so waiting longer adds nothing. Age 71 matters for a different reason: your RRSP must become a RRIF by December 31 of the year you turn 71, and forced withdrawals start the next year.

    How much bigger is CPP if I wait until 70?

    CPP rises 0.7% for each month after 65, so 42% more at 70. Starting at 60 cuts it by 0.6% a month, or 36%. The change is permanent, and the pension is indexed to inflation either way. The 2026 maximum at 65 is $1,507.65 a month, about $965 at 60 and about $2,141 at 70.

    How much bigger is OAS if I wait until 70?

    OAS rises 0.6% for each month after 65, so 36% more at 70 — about $1,037 a month instead of $762.50 (October–December 2026). It can’t start before 65. From age 75 it rises a further 10%.

    What is the OAS clawback threshold for 2026?

    $95,323 of net income. OAS is repaid at 15 cents for every dollar above it, collected by reducing OAS the following July to June. It’s calculated per person, not on a couple’s combined income.

    What is an RRSP meltdown?

    Withdrawing part of your RRSP in lower-income years — usually between retiring and starting CPP and OAS — so it’s taxed at lower rates now instead of higher rates later. A smaller RRSP means smaller forced RRIF withdrawals, which can keep income under the clawback threshold. It isn’t always worth it: if your later income stays low, it can mean paying tax sooner for nothing. Here, withdrawals start right away to pay the income you need, so you can compare a later CPP and OAS start paid for from your RRSP against starting earlier.

    What is the CPP breakeven age?

    The age at which the total received from starting later catches up with starting earlier. For a maximum pension, starting at 70 instead of 65 usually catches up in the early 80s before tax. The after-tax age can be different, which is why both are shown.

    Where do I find my CPP at 65?

    In My Service Canada Account, under your CPP Statement of Contributions and retirement estimate. That estimate assumes you keep contributing until 65 — if you stop working earlier, your real amount may be lower.
